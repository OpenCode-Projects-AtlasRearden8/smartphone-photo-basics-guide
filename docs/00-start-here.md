# Start Here

You already carry the camera. Tucked in your pocket, riding along to
the grocery store, waiting rooms, birthday dinners, and every ordinary
Tuesday — there is a camera good enough to make pictures worth keeping
for decades. This manual is about learning to use it well, with no new
gear, no jargon, and no lectures.

## The one big idea

A photo worth keeping is not the one with the most technical polish.
It is the one that makes you feel the moment again: the way the light
sat on your grandmother's shoulder, the exact chaos of a toddler's
birthday cake, the fog on the trail the morning everything felt calm.

Everything in this manual serves that one idea. We will talk about
light, steadiness, framing, and portraits — but every tip exists to
help you catch real moments, not to win a photography contest.

## How this manual works

4. **[Portraits that flatter](04-portraits-that-flatter.md)** — making

5. **[Organizing as you shoot](05-organizing-as-you-shoot.md)** — tiny

   friendly routine you can do in one sitting a month.
10. **[Glossary](glossary.md)** — every term we avoided, in case you

## What this is not

This is not a technical course. There are no f-stops here, no sensor
sizes, no comparison charts. There is nothing to buy — no tripod, no
lens, no subscription. If a tip in this manual requires a purchase,
the tip failed, and you may consider writing an angry note in the
glossary margins.

## A note on practice

Nobody reads their way to good photos, the same way nobody reads their
way to riding a bike. So the chapters end the same way: with one small
thing to try today. Pick the smallest one. Take three photos with it.
That is genuinely the whole assignment.

> The best camera is the one that is already in your pocket — and
> after these chapters, you will actually believe it.
