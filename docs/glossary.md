# Glossary

Plain-English explanations of every term a curious camera app will one
day throw at you, plus the few words this manual uses on purpose.

## Words this manual uses on purpose

**Open shade** — The shade on the *open-sky side* of whatever casts the
shadow: beside a building whose far side faces away from the sun, at
the awning's edge, under a tree with sky beyond it. Not pitch-dark;
just out of the sun's direct glare. The friendliest portrait light in
the world.

**Fill the frame** — Get close enough that your subject occupies most
of the photo instead of hovering in a sea of background.

**Keepers** — The tiny working album this manual suggests. One place
that collects your favorites for printing, gifting, and tough-day scrolling.

**The keeper star / heart / favorite** — Same idea, different apps:
double-tap the best shot of a moment. This single mark makes sorting
later nearly effortless.

**Burst** — Hold the shutter for a stretch of rapid-fire photos.
Perfect for motion and group laughs; delete all but one or two on the spot.

## Terms your phone may throw at you

**Aperture / f-stop** — How wide the tiny lens-door opens. Bigger
number = smaller door. Your phone picks it for you; nothing to learn,
this word just got in here through the keyhole.

**Bokeh** — Gentle background blur, traditionally from real lenses.
Portrait mode fakes this. Beautiful when subtle, scribbly around hair
and glasses when not — check before sharing.

**Compute photography** — The phone quietly fixing things in software
after each press: brightness stacking, steady-hand compensation,
color correction. Untouched, this is the superpower of phone photos.
Overridden by a badly-tuned "beauty" filter, it is what makes everyone
look like a mannequin.

**EV / exposure compensation** — The little sun icon with a slider
that lets you say "brighter" or "darker" to your phone. When in doubt,
slide up slightly for people, because easy-detail in shadows is a gift
you give yourself later.

**EXIF / metadata** — Information embedded silently in every photo:
date, time, location, device. Handy for sorting legitimately; check
it before sharing photos publicly from a sensitive location, since
"Some" social apps strip it out while others do not. Enabled/
disabled in camera settings, where possible.

**HDR** — High Dynamic Range: the phone takes several quick shots and
blends the bright window and the dark room into one doable photo.
Modern phones do HDR always-on and it is usually better that way.

**HEIC / HEIF** — A space-saving photo format modern phones shoot by
default (roughly half the size of JPEG at similar quality). The one
friction JPEG never had: *most* services understand it now, and
anything stuck can be set to "Most Compatible" (iPhone) or "JPEG"
(Android) in camera settings.

**Interval/timelapse** — Long stretches compressed: your phone takes
a photo every few seconds and bundles them into a short-ish video of
clouds or construction workers.

**ISO** — The camera's sensitivity to light, commonly a silent
visitor: low ISO in bright sun, high ISO in gloom. When ISO climbs,
photos gain a sparkle of grain called **noise**. All automatic — as
long as you stood in better light, of course.

**Live Photo / Motion Photo** — Still photo plus 1–3 seconds of
recorded motion and sound. Choose the single best *still* frame from
one (Photos lets you pick a key frame) when a burst feels too heavy.

**Optical vs. digital zoom** — Optical zoom uses a second lens and is
distance you can trust. Digital zoom is cropping in mid-air — keep it
mild (under 2–3×); when past that, take the wide shot and *crop*
later, which does the same thing with more control.

**Portrait mode** — See "bokeh." Great for people one to three steps
away; treacherous for fine edges at bigger distances.

**RAW** — A file saved exactly as the sensor saw it, for people who
plan to edit colors themselves. Off by default; unnecessary for this
manual, but fun for the curious.

**Shutter speed / long exposure** — How long the camera "looks" before
recording. This chapter's blur-creep villain: long looks + moving
hands = smear. Steady hands shorten the look.

**Wide / ultrawide / telephoto lens** — 1× is the main lens and the
sharpest; ultrawide (0.5×) exaggerates space and gets grand rooms; telephoto
(2–5×) is real zoom. Start photos at 1×, borrow the others on purpose.
