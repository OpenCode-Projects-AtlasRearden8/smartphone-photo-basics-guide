Printing Favorites

Photos on screens are rentals; prints are ownership. A 4×6 on the fridge outlives every phone you will ever own. This chapter turns favorites into paper with minimum effort and no fancy equipment.

Pick from the favorites, not from the roll

Because of the organizing habits, the hard part is already done: your best photos carry a heart or a star. When it is time to print, you start from the favorites list, not by scrolling four years of camera roll.

The rule that keeps printing joyful instead of a march: print fewer than you think. Ten excellent prints beat one hundred okay ones.

Three easy routes to paper

Route one: the pharmacy kiosk. Drugstores and big-box stores wireless-accept photos by app or website and have them ready in about an hour, usually well under thirty cents a print for 4×6. Bring the phone, order from the parking lot, pick up while buying milk. This is the fastest path from camera roll to fridge.

Route two: the mail-order print service. Upload favorites to a well-known print company, choose sizes, and a padded envelope arrives. Slower, but nicer paper choices and value packs; ideal for the monthly ritual.

Route three: the home printer — only if you already own it. Regular inkjet printers can do a pleasant 4×6 from any office-supply photo paper. Do not buy a printer for photos only; the pharmacy route is cheaper and they do it better.

Sizes that make sense

  • 4×6 — the workhorse. Fits every frame from the drugstore aisle, cheap, perfect for a shoebox, drawer, or wall.
  • 5×7 — a step up, right for gifts of portraits.
  • 8×10 or larger — only for the true keepers, and only if the photo is crisp when you zoom all the way in (see below).

The quality check before you spend money

Zoom all the way in on the candidate photo. If the faces stay sharp even when the photo is huge on screen, any size will print well. If it is mushy at that zoom, it still prints fine at 4×6 or smaller — phones are designed for pocket-size prints. Let a 4×6 be your default. The special frames will not hold a grudge.

Hint: photos that are worth enlarging usually have good light (see the light chapter yet again — light is everything).

Print with a caption or a date on the back (pencil only)

On the back of each print, write the date and the who/where in pencil. Pencil, never ink — ink presses through and bleeds over years. A decade from now, "Nana's 80th – backyard – before the cake" beats everything else, including your memory.

Where prints go to live

  • A small basket or box is a finished project — prints were meant to be riffled through like postcards.
  • Clipboards, washi tape, or a strand of twine with mini clips: a rotating wall that costs pocket change and can be re-curated monthly.
  • Actual frames for the few that earned spots. Buy cheap frames; you will keep swapping them as your favorites change, and that is precisely the point.
  • Books: photo-books make great once-a-year projects and even better gifts; the monthly ritual below feeds them neatly.

What prints fix in us

A photo in a shoebox gets shared: handed across the table, laughed at, passed to grandkids. It slows you down in the best way — no scrolling, no notifications, a rectangle of a specific afternoon. Two prints a month is enough to make this real. Which brings us to the ritual.
