The Shooting Checklist

One page. Keep it where you will find it: a photo of this page saved in your phone, or as a pinned note. Before a day of photos worth keeping, glance once — after a while it will live in your hands without any reading at all.

Before you leave the house (2 minutes)

  • Flash off as the default (re-enable only in emergencies).
  • Grid lines on in camera settings (they help, invisibly).
  • Beauty/face-smoothing filter off or toned down — real faces win, long-term.
  • Check space: a few hundred MB free beats a dead roll mid-birthday.
  • Backup working? If it has been a month or more, spot-check that yesterday's photos appear in your cloud/second copy.
  • Battery — the same charger trick everyone forgets: charge on the way, even briefly.

Before you press the button (2 seconds)

  • Light: subject facing a window or open shade; sun not overhead (or move them).
  • Tap the face on the screen so focus and exposure land on a person.
  • Edges: quick sweep for trash bins, stray elbows, poles-in-heads.
  • Background: calmer available in one step sideways or lower?
  • Horizon level — unless tilting on purpose.
  • Closer? The frame is always asking you to fill it.

For people (2 seconds more)

  • Camera at chest height or slightly higher, never chin-height-up.
  • Body a quarter-turn, hands with jobs.
  • Talk, then shoot at the end of the sentence — catch the real laugh.
  • A burst of 3–5 for groups, kids, and pets; pick the keeper on the spot.
  • Group of many on a timer: prop the phone, walk in, be the laughter.

In dim places (2 seconds)

  • Find the window or lamp first; move your subject, not your settings.
  • Two hands, elbows in, exhale, press.
  • Or 3-second timer — hands resting, not tapping.
  • Lean on anything: wall, bench, friend.

While you are in the moment (10 seconds total)

  • Favorite the keeper of each little moment — star today, not in February.
  • Delete near-duplicates on the spot, keep without guilt.
  • Rare-but-useful extras: photo of the sign, plaque, or serial number you would otherwise screenshot — the roll forgives it.

Coming home (90 seconds, in the car is fine)

  • Caption or album: name today with a real name ("Nana's 80th – backyard").
  • Move any screenshots to where they belong (or queue the weekly purge).
  • Drop the day's happiest three into Keepers.
  • Notice what you took; the noticing is literally the practice.

Print the top half of this page, fold it, and live with it. In two weekends this whole list compresses into three habits: move toward light, tap the face, favorite the keeper.
