The Monthly Print Ritual

One sitting a month, roughly twenty minutes, low light optional. This ritual quietly stitches together everything else in the manual: it rewards favorites, airs the backup, and ends with paper in your hands.

There is no failure state. If you miss two months, you do not owe money or progress — you simply do the next one. The ritual is meant to be low-energy and forgiving on purpose.

The ritual, step by step

1. Set the scene (2 minutes)

Tea, coffee, or whatever makes an hour tolerate moving slowly. Sit somewhere comfortable. Open your photo app on the sofa, not at a desk dressed like a tax appointment.

2. Sweep the month (5 minutes)

Scroll from the first of the month to today. Two passes:

  • Pass one — favorites check: did the best moments get their heart/star? Catch the strays now.
  • Pass two — pruning: delete the screenshots, the near-duplicate burst frames, and the accidental photos of your shoe. Ask about any unfavorite photo: "would I take it again?" If not, and it does not make you feel anything, let it go.

3. Name the month (3 minutes)

Make (or continue) an album named like a sky: "2026 – March." Or rename whatever the auto-album called it. Add the event captions you remember — one blank line per event, the car-ride two words, like "Easter at Sam's." You will not remember in two years; you do not need to.

4. Copy and confirm the backup (3 minutes)

  • Cloud users: open the backup on a second device or the web and confirm this month's photos are actually there. Monthly, once, 60 seconds. This is the single most protective minute in this manual.
  • Computer/drive users: do this month's copy now. Plugin, drag, done. Set the phone reminder for next month while the cable is warm.

5. Choose three prints (5 minutes)

Open Keepers (and the month's album) and pick three photos:

  1. one portrait of someone you love,
  2. one scene or place shot,
  3. one wildcard — funniest, weirdest, or best pet expression.

Three is a floor and a ceiling on purpose. It keeps the ritual joy-sized and makes the choosing an act of love rather than a chore of curation. Looking at the third's expression is allowed and encouraged.

6. Send them to paper (2 minutes now, paper later)

  • That moment: open your print app (pharmacy pickup or mail order), select the three, add a 5×7 if one photo is a gift, check out. Ninety seconds at a picnic table works.
  • Bonus energy version: order extras as flat gifts for the people in the photos. A mailed 4×6 with a pencil note on the back is a disproportionately wonderful thing to receive.

7. Close the ritual (1 minute)

When prints arrive, they go places on purpose: fridge, twine wall, a basket, a frame — or straight into the mail with an address on it. Append to the back in pencil: date, names, place.

Done. Nothing else is required until next month. If you did only step 4 every month and nothing more, your family photos would already be riskier-proof; if you do only step 5 every month, your walls would fill with recent joy. Everything else is a bonus.

Versions of the ritual for low-energy months

  • Five-minute version: steps 2 and 4 only. Sweep and confirm backup.
  • Cozy version: steps 5 and 7 only — choose prints, write a caption, put one print somewhere visible.
  • Cloud-only month: skip printing, just do step 4. The ritual bends so it does not break.

Once a year (optional, celebratory)

At the turn of the year, the twelve little albums become one photo-book (print route number two), or the year's favorites become a small wall gallery, or each kid gets a year's worth bound for their shelf. The point of the year-end isn't perfection; it is that by then, the twelve months already exist in tidy form, so the "big annual project" keeps taking twenty minutes instead of a full weekend of scrolling archaeology.
